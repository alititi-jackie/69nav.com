What Is OnlyFans? How It Works

What is OnlyFans and how does it work? Subscriptions, PPV, tips and the 80/20 split — a plain-English guide for fans and creators.

OnlyFans is a creator subscription platform where fans pay creators directly for exclusive content and interaction. Launched in 2016, it grew into one of the defining businesses of the creator economy — processing billions of dollars in fan spending each year.

Although it is widely associated with adult content, the platform itself is a neutral toolkit: subscriptions, pay-per-view unlocks, tips, and messaging that any creator — fitness coaches, musicians, chefs, or adult performers — can use. Here is how it actually works.

OnlyFans in One Paragraph

OnlyFans is a two-sided marketplace: creators publish photos, videos, live streams, and messages on a profile page; fans pay for access. The platform handles payments, hosting, chat, and age verification, while creators handle everything else — content, marketing, and fan relationships.

Scale is enormous: industry estimates put registered users in the hundreds of millions and annual fan spending around $7–8 billion, with several million active creators.

How Subscriptions Work

The core product is the monthly subscription:

  • Creators set their own price, typically between $4.99 and $49.99 per month (most newcomers start at $4.99–$9.99).
  • Subscribing unlocks the creator's included feed for the billing period — recurring access, not a one-time purchase.
  • Creators can also run free pages with no subscription fee, monetizing entirely through other means (see below).
  • Subscriptions auto-renew; fans can cancel from their settings at any time.

Important nuance: a subscription covers the creator's included content — not necessarily every message, custom request, or locked post.

Pay-Per-View (PPV), Tips and Custom Requests

Beyond subscriptions, creators monetize through:

  • PPV in direct messages — the most common form. A creator sends a locked photo, video, or message (individually or as a mass message) that the fan pays to unlock. Minimums start around $3; DM unlocks are capped (historically $100 per message).
  • PPV feed posts — individual locked posts on the main feed, more typical on free pages where PPV is the entire business model.
  • Tips — voluntary payments, often $5 and up, sent to show appreciation or get attention.
  • Custom requests — bespoke content priced creator-by-creator, frequently the highest-margin item a creator sells.

The 80/20 Split: Where the Money Goes

OnlyFans takes a flat 20% commission on subscriptions, PPV, and tips; creators keep 80%. This split has held since launch and has become the industry benchmark — Patreon, Fansly, and most newer creator platforms have converged on the same ratio.

Practical math: on a $50 PPV sale, the creator keeps $40 before taxes and payout fees.

For Fans: Getting Started Safely

  • OnlyFans runs in the browser — there is no official OnlyFans app for iPhone or Android. (OFTV, its free streaming product, does have apps; treat app-store lookalikes carefully.)
  • Use a unique password and enable two-factor authentication.
  • Check what a subscription actually includes before paying — locked DMs and PPV posts cost extra.
  • Review your statements: know the billing descriptor so you can spot unauthorized charges fast.

For Creators and Avoiding Scams

Creator signup requires government ID and age verification (18+), and approval can take a few days. Once live, common pitfalls include:

  • Impersonation scams — fake accounts pretending to be popular creators, or fake "OnlyFans support" messages asking for login details. OnlyFans will never ask for your password.
  • Off-platform payment lures — anyone pushing you to pay or get paid outside the platform is almost certainly running a scam.
  • Content theft — re-uploads of paid content are common; the platform offers takedown tools, and watermarking helps.
  • Remember the less glamorous side: income is taxable, chargebacks happen, and building an audience takes sustained marketing effort.

FAQ

Is OnlyFans only for adult content?
No. Adult content is a large part of its public image, but creators also run fitness, cooking, music, comedy, and coaching pages. The platform is a creator toolkit, not an adult-only site.
Is there an official OnlyFans app?
No. The main OnlyFans service runs in the browser on phones and computers. OFTV — OnlyFans' free streaming product — does have official apps. Be cautious of app-store imitations.
How much does OnlyFans take from creators?
A flat 20% commission on subscriptions, PPV, and tips. Creators keep 80% of every fan payment.
How can I avoid OnlyFans scams?
Never pay or share login details outside the platform, be skeptical of "free premium" offers and impersonator accounts, and verify creator links from their official social profiles.
