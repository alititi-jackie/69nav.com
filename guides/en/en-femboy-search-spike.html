"Femboy": Inside the 2025 Search Spike

"Femboy" jumped 15 places to #10 on Pornhub's 2025 rankings. What the term means and what the trend says about changing tastes.

The single biggest mover in Pornhub's 2025 Year in Review was "femboy" — up 15 places to land at #10 among the year's most-searched terms worldwide, cracking the global top ten for the first time.

This article explains what the term means, where it comes from, and what such a sharp spike suggests about shifting viewer tastes — in respectful, non-explicit terms.

The Stat: +15 Places to #10

Pornhub's 2025 global top ten read: hentai, MILF, pinay, lesbian, anal, big ass, latina, anime, asian, and femboy at #10 — the only genuinely new entrant, leaping 15 spots in a single year.

Pornhub itself described the surge as representative of "a global trend of more people searching for content showing feminine adult men." Double-digit jumps are rare in a chart where the top ranks barely move year to year, which is why this one drew so much coverage.

What Does "Femboy" Mean?

In current usage, "femboy" (a blend of "feminine" and "boy") refers to adult men who present femininely — through clothing, makeup, hairstyle, or mannerisms — without necessarily identifying as women or as transgender. It is primarily an aesthetic and fashion identity rather than a gender identity, though individuals use the term in personal ways.

The word lives mostly in online spaces: fashion communities, streaming culture, anime and gaming fandoms. As a search term, it clusters with interest in androgynous and gender-fluid presentation.

Roots: From Online Subculture to Mainstream Search

The term's path mirrors a familiar internet pattern:

  • Subculture origins — years of circulation in anime, gaming, and LGBTQ-adjacent online communities, where feminine male characters and creators were already popular.
  • Fashion crossover — androgynous menswear (skirts, thigh-highs, oversized streetwear) moving from niche forums into mainstream retail and social media.
  • Creator visibility — streamers and influencers whose feminine presentation reaches audiences far beyond adult sites.
  • Search normalization — once a term is widely understood, curiosity converts into searches; the 2025 spike likely marks that tipping point.

What the Spike Reflects

Analysts read the femboy surge as part of a broader diversification of taste. The 2025 data showed movement across the board — "latina" climbing in the US, "pinay" holding at #3 globally on the strength of Philippine traffic, anime-adjacent terms entrenched — suggesting viewers are exploring more varied categories than the historically static top ranks implied.

It also fits the industry's own commentary about audiences growing "bolder about naming what they actually like" — searching for specific, previously niche interests rather than defaulting to broad categories.

A Note on Respectful Language

A quick etiquette note, since this term describes real people: "femboy" is a self-applied label. Used descriptively and neutrally, it is fine; used to mock, misgender, or harass someone, it isn't. The same applies to any identity-adjacent search term — curiosity is normal, contempt is not.

And as with any trend piece built on one platform's data: Pornhub's rankings describe Pornhub's audience. They are a useful cultural signal, not a census of human desire.

FAQ

Not inherently — it is widely used as a self-identifier in fashion and online communities. Like any label, context matters: descriptive use is fine, mocking use is not.
Why did it spike specifically in 2025?
No single cause is proven, but the likely mix is subculture maturation, fashion crossover into the mainstream, greater creator visibility, and search normalization reaching a tipping point.
Is this trend US-only?
No. The #10 ranking is global. Regional breakouts differ (e.g., "latina" topped US searches), but the femboy surge registered worldwide.
