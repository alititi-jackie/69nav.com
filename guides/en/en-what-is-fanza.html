What Is FANZA (DMM)?

FANZA is Japan's largest official adult content platform, run by DMM. Learn how it works, how it differs from free streaming sites, and what to know before using it.

FANZA is the name you will encounter whenever Japan's legal adult industry comes up. It is the country's largest official platform for adult video — operated by the Japanese tech and retail giant DMM — with a catalog stretching into the tens of thousands of titles.

Unlike the free streaming sites most overseas viewers know, FANZA is a legitimate store: you buy or rent titles, the creators and studios get paid, and everything on it complies with Japanese law. Here is how it works and how it compares to the alternatives.

FANZA and DMM: What's the Relationship?

The two names are often used interchangeably outside Japan, but they are not quite the same thing. DMM is the parent company — a sprawling Japanese marketplace covering games, e-books, streaming video, and online services. FANZA is DMM's dedicated adult label, previously branded as DMM.R18 before the 2018 rename.

Think of it this way: DMM is the department store, FANZA is the adults-only floor. When people talk about buying official Japanese adult video online, they mean FANZA.

How FANZA Works

FANZA operates like a digital video store. You create an account, browse the catalog by studio, series, performer, or genre, and then either purchase a title outright or rent it for streaming. Purchased videos can also be downloaded through DMM's official DMM Player software, which uses DRM protection to prevent redistribution.

The catalog covers mainstream studio releases, independent labels, and amateur-style series. Because everything is licensed, you get reliable metadata — accurate titles, studio names, and release dates — which is a sharp contrast to the mislabeled uploads common on pirate sites. FANZA also runs subscription-style unlimited viewing plans for heavy viewers.

FANZA vs Free Streaming Sites

The core difference is the business model. Free streaming sites monetize through advertising — often aggressive pop-ups and redirects — and typically host unlicensed uploads. FANZA charges you directly, which means no malvertising, no fake download buttons, and no mislabeled files.

Quality is another gap: FANZA sells high-bitrate streams and downloads, while free sites usually recompress uploads. And there is an ethical dimension — on FANZA, studios and performers are compensated for their work. On pirate streaming sites, they are not.

The trade-off is cost and convenience. Free sites ask nothing upfront; FANZA requires an account, payment, and — for most overseas users — some setup to handle regional restrictions (see below).

Access and Payment Basics

FANZA is built for the Japanese domestic market, which creates friction for international users. The platform is geo-restricted: you generally need a Japan-region account, and its systems actively detect and block many VPN and datacenter IP addresses. In early 2026 the platform further tightened content protection, breaking most screen-recording tools.

Payment runs on DMM Points, a prepaid credit system. International credit cards are frequently declined at checkout; many overseas buyers instead purchase DMM Points through PayPal, which tends to process more reliably. Expect the interface to be primarily in Japanese, though browser translation handles most of it.

Things to Know Before You Start

A few practical notes. First, beware of copycat sites: FANZA's popularity has spawned lookalike domains that harvest payment details — always verify you are on the genuine DMM/FANZA domain. Second, downloads are DRM-locked to the official player; you are buying a license to watch, not an open video file. Third, check your local laws regarding adult content purchases and age verification before signing up.

Used as intended, FANZA is the safest and most reliable way to access Japanese adult video — it is simply built for a domestic audience, so international users should expect a little setup.

Why was it called DMM.R18 before?
DMM rebranded its adult division from DMM.R18 to FANZA in 2018 to give the adult business its own distinct identity separate from the main DMM brand.
Is FANZA free?
No. It is a paid store: individual purchases, rentals, and subscription plans. Free "FANZA" content found elsewhere is pirated or a scam.
